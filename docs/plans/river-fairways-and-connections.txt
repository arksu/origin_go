DEEP FAIRWAYS AND GAMEPLAY CONNECTIONS
======================================
2026-09-29 | OPTION B selected, with sparse deep-water tributaries.

NON-NEGOTIABLE
==============
Every main river, tributary, and narrow water connector has a continuous
deep-water fairway. A tributary may be narrower than a main river, but it
must NEVER become shallow-only or taper below the boat passage clearance.
A deep tile chain alone is insufficient if the boat cannot fit through it.

The user selected a minimum of 3 deep tiles on 2026-09-29. The generator
protects a continuous sequence of 3x3 deep-water placements, including bends,
junctions, lake entrances, and tributary ends. This is configurable and is
not a claim of runtime boat collision testing.

Tributaries are allowed but infrequent. Preserve long unbranched stretches
and irregular spacing between junctions. Control their density separately
from curve roughness and from narrow connectors needed for player travel.
Every accepted tributary retains the same deep-fairway passage requirement.

C marks the eastern deep-water connector between the two main routes.
P marks an optional portage between the northern branch and southern river.
Players can sail around through C, or carry the boat at P as a shortcut.
This is an illustrative layout, not a required ring or prescribed map shape.
The branch count in this small sketch is not the target production density;
the selected direction calls for sparse branches across the whole map.

Important distinctions:
- Main routes and branches are chosen for useful player destinations.
- A river can join another river directly; a lake is not needed at each join.
- Connections can form cycles. No simulated watershed has to permit them.
- There is no world-center source and no mandatory exit toward the border.
- Rivers do not all have to connect directly to each other.
- Narrow channels can connect neighboring routes while looking distinct.
- Portages are explicit opportunities, not excuses for broken fairways.
- A river endpoint can have an inland pool or dock with boat clearance;
  it does not have to become a shallow stream or reach an ocean.

Branch density is a separate design control from curve roughness. Increasing
small-bend detail should not automatically create hundreds of tributaries.
Use a low tributary budget plus junction spacing, not a branch at every bend
or lake. Tune the numeric limits in previews; no specific count is selected.

A narrow connector can reduce the bank margin or the total river width.
It cannot reduce the deep passage below the required boat clearance.
No shallow ford, sand bar, or decorative island may block a required route.


4. GENERATION AND ACCEPTANCE CONTRACT
=====================================
Generate in this order:
1. Choose gameplay endpoints, connections, and route roles.
   Reserve a separate sparse budget and spacing for optional tributaries.
2. Shape the centerline using Option B's bends within bends.
3. Sweep a sufficiently wide DEEP corridor along the entire path.
4. Join deep corridors at confluences, connectors, and lake entrances.
5. Add optional irregular banks outside the protected deep corridor.
6. Preserve that corridor through biome, terrain, and shoreline processing.
7. Validate boat movement on the FINAL tiles, not only the abstract graph.

Validate:
- Tributaries stay infrequent and spaced apart, with substantial unbranched
  reaches. Increasing bend detail does not increase tributary frequency.
- Every generated river segment has deep boat clearance end to end,
  including tributaries and narrow connectors.
- Test the actual boat footprint and movement/collision rules, especially
  at diagonal steps, tight bends, junctions, and entrances to lakes.
- No route relies on diagonal corner-touching water tiles.
- Intended village docks and linked water regions remain reachable by boat.
- No accidental self-intersection or bank pinch creates an unwanted shortcut
  or blocks the intended route.
- Portage endpoints are usable land access points and are marked separately
  from navigable water.
- The same seed reproduces the same result.

If a proposed bend fails clearance, reshape or widen it; if necessary reject
and regenerate that route. Never silently downgrade it to shallow water.

These are requirements for the next implementation. The rollback restores
the previous generator; it is not evidence that these checks already pass.
